Research Foundation
What the Science Says

This programme is built on current peer-reviewed consensus from sports science, including work by Schoenfeld, Krieger, and the ISSN. Here's what the evidence actually supports.

📊

Volume is King

Research confirms a clear dose-response: more weekly sets → more hypertrophy. Aim for 10–20 direct sets per muscle group per week. Keep sessions under 16 sets to avoid diminishing returns.

🔁

Frequency 2×/Week

Hitting each muscle group twice per week is the research sweet spot for hypertrophy. The key driver is total weekly volume — frequency is just the vehicle to distribute it effectively.

🎯

Rep Range Flexibility

Hypertrophy occurs across 6–30 reps when effort is high. A mix of 6–10 (strength) and 10–15 (metabolic) sets is optimal. Technique and proximity to failure matter more than exact rep counts.

⚡

Progressive Overload

Measurable growth begins after ~4 weeks of consistent training. Systematically increasing load, reps, or sets over time is the non-negotiable driver of long-term adaptation.

💤

Recovery is Training

Muscle protein synthesis peaks 5–24h post-session. Sleep (7–9h), rest days on weekends, and managing session intensity are as important as the training itself for returners.

🔬

RIR — Reps in Reserve

Training within 1–3 reps of failure provides maximal stimulus. This programme starts at RIR 3–4 (comfortable challenge) and builds to RIR 1 (near-maximal effort) by Week 12.

Programme Architecture
3-Phase Progression

Each phase builds on the last. Do not skip ahead — the foundation weeks prevent injury and build the neuromuscular efficiency needed for the high-intensity phases.

01

Phase 1

Weeks 1–4 · Foundation
3 sets per exercise
12–15 reps — moderate load
90 sec rest between sets
RIR 3–4 — controlled effort
Focus on perfect form, mind-muscle connection, and tissue adaptation. Start lighter than you think you should.
02

Phase 2

Weeks 5–8 · Build
4 sets per exercise
8–12 reps — moderate-heavy load
75 sec rest between sets
RIR 2–3 — genuine challenge
Increase weight on all main lifts by Week 5. Add one compound superset per session.
03

Phase 3

Weeks 9–12 · Intensify
4 sets + drop set on last
6–10 reps — heavy
60 sec rest between sets
RIR 1–2 — near-maximal
Final set of main exercises: drop 30% weight, rep to near-failure. This is where the real gains consolidate.

Research Note on Timing
The "anabolic window" (the idea you must consume protein within 30 minutes post-workout) has been largely debunked by meta-analyses. Current evidence shows total daily protein intake is the primary driver of hypertrophy. That said, consuming protein in a wider 2-hour window post-workout still aids recovery. A pre-sleep casein dose (20–40g) has consistent evidence for overnight MPS support — this one is worth doing.
The Long Game
Staying Motivated & Tracking Progress

Motivation is finite. Systems are permanent. Here's how to stay on track when the initial excitement fades — which it will, usually around Week 3.

01

Log Every Session

Write down weight, sets, reps. Seeing last week's numbers in front of you creates automatic competition with your past self. A simple notebook or your phone notes work fine. No app required.

02

The +1 Rule

Each week, aim to do one more rep OR add the smallest weight increment (1–2 kg) to at least one exercise. One small win per week = 52 improvements per year. This is progressive overload made practical.

03

Monthly Photo Check-In

Take a photo on the same day each month, same lighting, same angle. Mirrors lie. Photos don't. Progress is slower than you'd like — monthly photos prevent you from quitting too early.

04

Measure What Matters

Track: chest circumference, upper arm (flexed), shoulder width, bodyweight. Measure every 4 weeks. Scale weight alone is misleading — muscle gain and fat loss can net to zero on the scale.

05

Never Miss Twice

Missing one session is human. Missing two in a row is a pattern. Life will interrupt — travel, illness, kids. The rule is simple: whatever happens, never let a gap exceed one missed session before you return.

06

Anchor the Habit

Train at the same time each day. Link it to an existing habit (morning coffee → training). The session shouldn't require a decision — it's just what you do after X. Decision fatigue is the enemy of consistency.

07

Celebrate Phase Completions

Finishing 4 weeks is a real achievement. Mark it. Plan Phase 2. The structure of a 12-week programme gives you natural milestones — use them as motivational resets, not as endpoints.

08

Sleep is Your Cheat Code

7–9 hours is not optional. Testosterone drops significantly with under 6 hours sleep. Recovery, motivation, and muscle protein synthesis are all sleep-dependent. Protect it as fiercely as your training time.

Progress Tracking Checklist — What to Record

Weekly (every session)

Exercise name · Weight used · Sets completed · Reps per set · How you felt (1–10)

Every 4 Weeks

Body measurements (chest, arm, waist) · Progress photos · Scale weight (average over 7 days)

At Phase End (Wk 4 / 8 / 12)

Max reps test on key lifts · Compare Phase 1 weights to current · Assess form with phone video

Subjective Markers

Sleep quality · Energy levels · Recovery rate between sessions · Mood and stress load
